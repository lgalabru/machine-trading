# machine-trading 0.11.0: design

0.10.0's winners beat both tasks by reading how their scenarios were made,
not by trading the market. 0.11.0 closes those leaks, renames the tasks
after the skill they measure, adds OpenAI models, and fixes how a batch is
planned and reported. Nothing is tuned to a model's score: each change
removes a tell, and the ladder checks the families still rank strategies the
way a trader would.

## What 0.10.0's winners did

Read from the trials' session details (`bench/results/0.10.0`), and from
the bots' own comments.

**flash-crash.** Opus's bot names three edges in its comments: riding
moves that land in equal steps (Phoenix moves its price at most 4% a step,
and the grader cut bigger at-once moves into equal ones), fading fizzles,
and fading shocks. The comments overstate the first. Graded on a fresh set
of the 0.11 family with equal steps already gone, it still made 1.8× the
reference, profitable in 86% of sessions (Fable's: 2.2×, 83%), past the
ladder's 75% limit and above the regime oracle (1.22×). Attributed by its
own labels, fading what it took for fizzles made $9.0k of its $15k gross,
riding legs $3.9k, fading the aftermath $0.4k. Fizzles gave back all of
their move, to the exact level they left, and nothing but a fizzle can move
the mark before the shock starts: a fade once a fizzle landed was nearly
riskless. The regime oracle never trades fizzles, so it was no ceiling for
this edge.

**market-making.** Informed orders were drawn at 2–4× noise sizes, with
limits at 2–4× the competitor half-spread: each one stood out the moment it
traded. All three Opus runs flagged them and took the position cap with each
burst: $84–117 a jump session against the reference's $4.30, near flat in
calm and trending markets, at $7.5–9 of inference a run. The flow oracle (told
which orders are informed) was meant as the ceiling for this; the per-order
tell put it within reach of a few lines of code.

### Why Opus beat Fable

Same tell, different discipline. Fable's runs rode the bursts too (+$82 a
jump session) but also chased fake-outs, losing $11–25 a session in calm and
trending markets, and one Fable run never found the tell (skill 1.3×). Among
Opus runs, the lowest (14.4×) sent 12.7 market orders a session against 1.7–4.1
for the others, paying the spread on each. Fable runs cost $19–22 against
Opus's $7.5–9. The spread between models measured who found a leak and traded
it tidily, which is why both leaks close.

## Changes

### The tasks are named for the skill

`phoenix-flash-crash` and `phoenix-market-making` become `flash-crash-spcx`
and `market-making-spcx`: the skill, then the instrument. Phoenix is how the
market runs, not what's measured. `mtx bench review` and `compare` key a
task by its skill (`flash-crash`, `market-making`), so 0.10.0 and 0.11.0
results line up across the rename.

### Big moves land in unequal parts (flash-crash)

A move bigger than about 3.8% lands, still at once, in two or more parts on
consecutive steps, of random unequal sizes, sometimes one part more than the
fewest that fit. The first part no longer says how many follow. The
instruction says so in a clause. Ramps were already unequal.

### Fizzles give back a share that varies (flash-crash)

A fizzle now gives back a uniform 40% to 100% of its move, and the stretch
goes on around where it settled. Fading one is still mean reversion that
pays on average, the way fading a false start does, but no longer to a
known level, and a partial giveback costs a fade that waits for all of it.
The instruction gives the range. Fizzle odds, sizes and holds are unchanged.

### A regime oracle that trades (the ladder)

With fizzles fixed, the 0.10.0 bots made 1.27× and 1.51× the reference on a
fresh set, while the regime oracle made 1.00×: told each session's shape, it
only took the extreme-to-end trade and sat out the rest. A bot that rode
legs and faded aftermaths better than the reference beat it honestly, so
"below the regime oracle" no longer separated reading the generator from
trading well. The oracle now trades as the reference does and uses the
shape where the reference guesses: it holds a shock to its extreme, trades
from the extreme to where the aftermath ends, and trades fizzles as the
reference does. Its lead over the reference is what knowing the regime adds
to good trading. Changing the family again until the frozen bots fail
would fit the scenarios to two bots.

### Informed orders look like noise (market-making)

Each informed order now draws its size, limit and account exactly as a noise
order does: only its side and its timing (6 to 14 orders, within 6 to 30
slots of the jump) set it apart. Fake-outs are drawn exactly like informed
bursts, zero to two a session in any regime, about as common as announced
jumps (0.85 a session). Reading the tape still pays: a burst is more one-sided
volume than noise makes, but telling it apart takes statistics over several
orders, and half the bursts lead nowhere. The flow oracle stays the ceiling.

### OpenAI models, each in its own harness

`just bench` takes `provider/model`. Anthropic models run in Claude Code
(2.1.312), OpenAI models in Codex (0.166.0): each provider's own coding
agent, as its users would run it, pinned, with web search off and no memory
across trials. A shared third-party harness would compare models more
narrowly, but measures them in a harness neither was tuned for; results name
the harness with the model.

### A planned batch

`just bench` writes `batch.json` before the first trial: models, each one's
harness and version, attempts, the time limit, the metric, and the commit.
What was planned is what's reported; a batch that changes its plan is a new
batch.

- **Attempts: 5 per model and task** (was 3). Attempts vary far more than
  sessions: 0.10.0's three Fable market-making runs scored 1.3×, 10.1× and
  12.3×.
- **The metric stays skill**, total PnL over the hidden set divided by the
  reference's on the same sessions, mean over attempts. Within a batch every
  trial shares one hidden set, so skill and reward rank models identically;
  skill reads as "times the reference". Across releases it moves with the
  reference's PnL on each set, so compare views are read as rankings, not
  ratios.
- **90% intervals.** The leaderboard and model chips show a percentile
  bootstrap interval of the mean over attempts (three or more).
- **Time.** Scores are "within the task's agent time limit" (one hour, at
  `limit=1`), the same for every model.

### Practice runs faster

`mtx-practice` grades three sessions at a time (`MTX_JOBS` to change). The
world waits for the bot each step, so a session grades the same however
many run beside it. In 0.10.0, 11 of 18 runs spent their last stretch
waiting on sequential practice grades. The hidden grade is unchanged (one
bot session at a time). Practice sets are redrawn from the new families
(seed 110) and recalibrated.

## Checks

- Unequal parts alone didn't close flash-crash: the 0.10.0 bots stayed
  past both limits (above). The fizzle change followed from the
  attribution, not from a score.
- The 0.10.0 winners join the exploit checks (`bench/checks/exploits/*-0.10`)
  and the ladders: on the new families they must lose often enough and stay
  under the oracles.
- `mtx-core` tests: big moves land in unequal parts that each fit a step;
  informed orders' median size and limit are within 15% of noise's; fake-outs
  are about as common as announced jumps; about 85% of jumps are announced;
  fizzles give back a share that varies.

## Ladder results

Two fresh sets of 36 sessions each, on the families above and the new
regime oracle (`target/ladder-0.11`, 2026-11-05).

**flash-crash.** Every order rule holds on both sets with bootstrap odds of
1.0: blind bets, the dip buyer and momentum all lose to the reference, and
reference < regime oracle < hindsight. The regime oracle makes 1.88× and
3.14× the reference (2.51× mean); the exploit bots make 0.72× (opus 0.6.0),
1.49× (fable 0.6.0), 1.85× (opus 0.10.0) and 1.50× (fable 0.10.0), all under
it. One limit misses: `flash-crash-opus-0.10` is profitable in 55 of 72
sessions (76%) against a limit of 75%, one session over; on 0.10.0's family
it was at 83–86%. The miss is recorded, not tuned away: another change to
the family for one session of one frozen bot would fit the scenarios to it.

**market-making.** The exploit limits all hold: the 0.10.0 bots make 1.29×
(opus) and 4.05× (fable) the reference, far under the flow oracle's 38×,
and the fable bot is profitable in 86% of sessions against a limit of 90%.
The order rules fail: the naive quoter beats the reference on one set
(1.11×) and the bootstrap odds of each rule are about 0.65. The reference
makes $91 and $48 over 36 sessions ($1.3–2.5 a session), too little to
rank the honest rungs at that count. 0.9.0 calibrated these rules at 72
sessions, the task's hidden set, where the reference's lead held in 90% of
resamples; whether it still does on the new family, where informed orders
no longer stand out one at a time, is being measured at 72 sessions before
0.11.0 ships. A reference a naive quoter can match isn't an anchor, so if
it doesn't hold, the market-making reference needs a stronger edge, tuned on
practice draws of the family, not on any model's results.

## Not changed

Mandates, hidden set sizes (72), references, scoring, the grader and the
turn protocol. A change to any of those would move every score at once and
hide what the leaks were worth.
